The citation test

We open by asking an AI for five peer-reviewed references and checking them. In a 2023 study (Walters & Wilder, Scientific Reports), 55 % of the citations written by GPT-3.5 and 18 % of those written by GPT-4 were fabricated, and many of the real ones had wrong authors, years or pages. Newer models do better; the rule does not change: every reference is opened by a human.

The map: from AI to agents

  • Artificial intelligence (AI) — systems doing tasks that normally need human intelligence.
  • Machine learning (ML) — AI that learns patterns from data instead of hand-written rules.
  • Deep learning (DL) — ML with many-layer neural networks.
  • Generative AI (GenAI) — models that create new text, images, code or audio.
  • Large language models (LLMs) — generative AI for text, trained to predict the next token.
  • Agentic AI — an LLM plus tools (search, code, browser, files) and a loop: goal → plan → act → observe → check.

An LLM writes one token at a time, choosing what is plausible — not what is true. That is why it can hallucinate: fluent, confident and wrong.

Agents fail in new ways

  • Prompt injection — instructions hidden in a web page, PDF or email that the agent may obey.
  • Acting on errors — an invented fact becomes an email sent or a file overwritten.
  • Data leakage — unpublished results pasted into a tool whose terms you never read.
  • Automation bias — trusting a report because it looks thorough.

Prompt engineering

Six parts of a good prompt: role (who it acts as) · task (one clear verb) · context (topic, audience, your material) · format (length, sections) · examples (one or two) · constraints (what not to do; cite sources; say “I don’t know”). Then iterate: one change at a time. Ask for critique before prose, and ask it to ask you questions first.

Generating images

Describe the subject, style, composition, colours and the labels you need. Then check: AI images often garble text, draw impossible parts and invent data in charts. Use them only for explanatory figures — never as a result, measurement or photo of an experiment — and say in the caption that the image is AI-generated.

Ten habits

  1. Give context: who you are, the goal, the audience, the format.
  2. Give it your material instead of asking it to recall facts.
  3. Ask for structure and critique first, prose last.
  4. Iterate: one change per prompt; keep what works.
  5. Verify every fact, number and reference at the source.
  6. Protect data: nothing confidential or unpublished in unknown tools.
  7. Ask for uncertainty: “what are you least sure about?”
  8. Keep a log of the prompts and outputs that shaped your work.
  9. Rewrite in your own words — if you can’t explain it, it isn’t yours yet.
  10. Declare your use.

Allowed tools in this course: TECgpt, Agent Studio, Gemini, Copilot (institutional); Zotero, Mendeley for references.

In class and after class

Activity 1 (individual, 40 min): with any allowed tool (TECgpt, Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot), write a short text on a topic you care about and generate one visual for it — then check it. Activity 2 (graded, 5 %): present it to the group (3 minutes). Activity 3: reflection — a tool that amplifies your thinking, or a shortcut that replaces it?

Independent work (Canvas): Task 1 — brainstorm and research (individual, 5 %); Task 2 — drafting and visuals with AI (individual, 10 %).
