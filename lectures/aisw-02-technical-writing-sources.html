Principles of technical and scientific writing

  1. Clarity — one idea per sentence, one topic per paragraph.
  2. Precision — numbers with units and uncertainty; no “very” or “a lot”.
  3. Concision — cut every word that adds nothing.
  4. Objectivity — claims supported by data or a cited source.
  5. Reproducibility — another person could repeat what you did.
  6. Defined terms — define on first use; spell out acronyms.
  7. Consistent style — tense, units, citation format.
  8. Honesty — limitations stated; AI use declared.

Vague: “The robot works very well and is much faster.” Technical: “The prototype cleaned a 1.6 m² panel in 94 s (n = 10, SD 6 s), 31 % faster than manual cleaning.” (Illustrative numbers — every number comes from your data or a cited source, never from the AI.)

Scope, objective and structure

Review (organises what others found) · research (your method, data and results) · brief summary (one result, compact). State the scope (what is in and out) and an objective with a checkable verb. Usual structure, IMRaD: title, abstract (150–250 words), introduction (context → gap → objective), methods, results, discussion, conclusions, references. Formats: IEEE and Springer Nature guidelines (Canvas, self-study).

Searching and evaluating sources

Where: Biblioteca Digital Tec (Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect), Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, SciELO and Redalyc for research in Spanish. Search in English too. A search string: ("solar panel" OR photovoltaic) AND (cleaning OR soiling) AND robot*.

Six questions: does it exist (DOI opens)? who wrote it? where (peer-reviewed, not predatory)? when? evidence? does it say that (you read the results)? An AI answer is not a source — the source is what it points to, after you open it.

The literature matrix

One row per source: reference (with DOI) · main idea · method · key result · how it helps your article · verified. Group by theme, not by source, and summarise in your own words.

Editing and integrating with AI

Fine: spelling, grammar, readability, translation you check (technical terms are where tools fail — in mechanics “stress” is esfuerzo). Declare: restructured or rewritten text. To integrate sources, give the AI only your abstracts, ask it to group them and cite the source number for every point — then open each source and check.

In class and after class

Activity 4 (individual): define your topic — topic, question, objective, type, audience — and validate it. Activity 5 (individual): focus with AI, search, select at least 5 sources (≥ 3 peer-reviewed, ≥ 1 in English), fill the matrix. Activity 6 (graded, 5 %): present your sources, 3 minutes — Matrícula_Actividad09.pptx.
