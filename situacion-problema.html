Situación problema

teams of 2 Nov 6 · 13 · 20 final Nov 27, individual

In one sentence. Pick a business problem and a real dataset; by the end of November, have a Python program that loads it, describes it, plots the answers to your own questions — and explain your code out loud.

15 % of the course. Teams of two for three deliverables; the final delivery is individual. Formal release: Friday 16 October. This page is published early so nobody is surprised.

What it is

You pick a business problem and a real dataset, and by the end of November you have a Python program that loads the data, describes it statistically, and plots the answers to questions you wrote yourself — plus a short report that says what the numbers mean and what the business should do about it. Everything taught between now and then is a piece of that program: conditionals (week 2), functions (week 2), loops (weeks 3–4), lists and plots (weeks 7–8), data frames (week 9).

The method (the academia’s five steps)

  1. Define the problem. Pick an area you actually care about — the point is a program that processes a lot of data quickly, so pick data you want answers from.
  2. Collect the data. A table with quantitative and qualitative columns, from a real, current source.
  3. Clean it. Uniform dates and numbers, no duplicates, no useless rows.
  4. Analyse. Descriptive statistics and plots in Python.
  5. Conclude. What the results mean, and what you recommend.

The four deliveries

All four go to Canvas → Modules → Situación problema. Each missed or late stage costs 5 points off the project grade. No email submissions.

The final program — what it must contain

Written as functions plus a main(), the way every program in this course is written from week 2:

  • estadistica_descriptiva(tablero) — takes the DataFrame; computes at least five descriptive statistics related to your driving questions.
  • grafica1(tablero) — a simple bar or line chart.
  • grafica2(tablero) — a figure with two sub-plots, or one x against two y’s, or a groupby chart. Every chart with title, axis labels and legend.
  • menu() — displays: 1 Descriptive statistics · 2 Chart 1 · 3 Chart 2 · 4 Exit.
  • main() — loads the Excel into a DataFrame, shows the menu, runs the chosen function with if / elif / else, repeats with a while until Exit. All input() lives here.
  • A last line that calls main(). Comments before every function saying what it does.

The report — what it must contain

  1. Cover with your data and the project’s.
  2. The business and what it does.
  3. What each column means.
  4. The driving questions, answered.
  5. Screenshots: the statistics output, the two charts.
  6. Conclusion: what the analysis shows, and a strategic proposal — concrete actions the business should take, grounded in the data.

How it is graded

Rubric published before deliverable 1 (Friday 16 October, with the formal release). The shape: each deliverable has its own small rubric; the final weighs the program (functions that work and are yours), the report, and the oral explanation. A program you cannot explain is not yours.

What to do now

Nothing formal until 16 October. Informally: start noticing data. The best projects come from a dataset a student already cared about — a family business, a sport, a city problem. If you find one before October, bring it to asesorías.
