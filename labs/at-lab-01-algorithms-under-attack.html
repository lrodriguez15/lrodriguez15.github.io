Algorithms under attack

algorithms paper only Fri 25 Sep · 16:30–17:30 · Room 4206

Objective. Execute someone else's algorithm literally until it breaks, then write one that provably stops. Bring your laptop (20-minute diagnostic), a notebook, a pencil, and Tuesday's algorithm. Nothing is submitted to Canvas — hand your paper to Luis before you leave. PDF version.

Why this lab is paper-only

Our lab runs one hour before the Friday session that teaches Python, so nobody is asked to write Python today. This lab gives Tuesday’s idea a second, harder pass and hands you into the Friday lecture with a concrete failure fresh in mind. Coding starts in Lab 2 (Friday 2 October).

Exercise 1 — Break someone else’s algorithm

Pairs. 15 minutes.

  1. Swap your Tuesday price-validation algorithm with your partner.

  2. Execute theirs literally, on paper, for each of these four inputs — writing down what their steps actually produce, not what you know is meant:

    Input
    a 50
    b 0
    c -3
    d hola

  3. For every input where their algorithm does something wrong, undefined, or unstated, write one line naming the step number and the problem.

Hand back. You now have a bug report on your own algorithm, written by someone who was not trying to be kind to it.

The point: nobody’s Tuesday version survives all four. Input 0 is the interesting one — “positive” either includes it or does not, and almost nobody said which.

Exercise 2 — The algorithm that must terminate

Individual. 17 minutes.

A vending machine holds a drink costing 12 pesos. It accepts coins of 1, 2, 5 and 10 pesos, one at a time. It must keep accepting coins until the customer has paid enough, then release the drink and return any change.

Write the algorithm. INPUT: / OUTPUT: lines, numbered steps.

Then answer, in writing, underneath:

  • Where exactly does your algorithm stop? Point at the step number.
  • What does it do if the customer pays 12 exactly?
  • What does it do if the customer puts in a 10 and then walks away?

You do not have the tools to write this as a program yet — that is fine. This is the algorithm you will be able to write as a program in Week 3, and I want it on paper first.

Submission

Hand in, before you leave (to Luis, the TA):

  • Your two handwritten algorithms.
  • Your partner’s bug report on your Exercise 1 (with your name on it).
  • Exercise 2’s three written answers.
